🤯 What if you bet on ALL 6/58 combinations?

With the Ultra Lotto 6/58 jackpot nearing a billion pesos, this question may cross your mind: "What if I bet on every possible combination?" Short answer: discard that thought while you still can. You are not prepared for this task. Here is why.
1. How many combinations are there?
A lotto draw ignores order and does not allow repeats, so we use combinations:
nCr = n! / (r! (n − r)!)
where n is the number of choices and r is how many you pick.
58C6 = 58! / (6! × 52!) = 40,475,358 combinations
2. What does it cost?
40,475,358 × ₱24 per play = ₱971,408,592. Good luck earning that.
₱24 = ₱20 base + 20% Documentary Stamp Tax. Confirm the current price at your outlet.
3. Taxes eat the jackpot
Even if you win, prizes above ₱10,000 are taxed 20% (TRAIN Law), so you keep 80%. Example: a ₱907,000,000 jackpot leaves you ₱725,600,000, a loss of about ₱245.8 million against your ₱971.4M stake. Not a wise decision...
...for now. Break-even is ₱971,408,592 ÷ 0.8 = about ₱1.214 billion. Above that it becomes profitable, but still not a good idea.
4. The teller problem
One teller would have to enter 40,475,358 combinations. At 4 combinations per ticket that is 10,118,840 tickets. (OC folks would have a field day.)
5. The paper
A 4-combination ticket is about 3.25 inches long, so 10,118,840 tickets use about 32,886,230 inches (2,740,519 feet, roughly 835 km) of thermal paper. At 0.5 g per ticket you would carry about 5,059 kg (5.06 metric tons) of tickets, then sort and organize them to find the winner.
6. The time
Entering 10,118,840 tickets, error-free:
• 25 sec/ticket: 252,971,000 sec = 70,269.7 hours = 2,928 days
• 20 sec/ticket: 202,376,800 sec = 56,215.8 hours = 2,342 days
• 15 sec/ticket: 151,782,600 sec = 42,161.8 hours = 1,757 days
7. Do it in one day?
With 24 hours you need 2,928 tellers. Outlets are not open all day, so use 12 hours: 5,856 tellers. Rotate one hour per twelve and you need 488 reserves, for 6,344 people (and a lot of space).
Pay each ₱1,000 including food: ₱6,344,000. Spare change next to the bet itself.
Not enough manpower? Spread it over 2 days (Mon–Tue or Sat–Sun): about 3,172 people. Over 3 days (Wed–Fri): about 2,115 people.
Recap
You need at least ₱980 million, up to about 6,400 people and machines, a mountain of thermal paper, a very long time, and a meticulous, organized and slightly delusional mind.
And if someone else also hits the jackpot, you split the pot and the whole scheme goes awry. That has to hurt.

❓ Help

Getting started
  1. Choose a game with the tabs on Home (6/58, 6/55, 6/49, 6/42 or 6D). Then pick your numbers in Your Ticket (six numbers, or six digits for 6D), or tap 🍀 Lucky Pick for a random combination.
  2. Tap Draw Ball to draw one ball at a time, or Auto Draw 6 to draw all six.
  3. Your ticket is checked against the drawn balls and any prize is shown under the number grid.
  4. Tap 💾 Save to keep a ticket in My Tickets, and tap New Game to start over.

How do I claim a real prize?

Bring the original, undamaged winning ticket and two valid IDs, and claim within 1 year of the draw date. Prizes of ₱20 to ₱10,000 can be claimed at any authorized lotto outlet or PCSO branch office. Larger prizes need a PCSO office; see pcso.gov.ph.

How do I save a photo of my ticket?

Sign in first, then open My Tickets and tap Take photo of ticket. Photos are saved to your account on this device only.

Do I need an account?

No. You can play without signing in. If you sign up, your account is stored on this device only.

Who can play?

You must be at least 18 years old to buy lotto tickets or claim winnings.

Does it work offline?

Connect to the internet once to load the 3D machine. After that it works offline. Always confirm official results at pcso.gov.ph.
